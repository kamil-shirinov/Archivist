          Box 14/Accuracy report

          Accuracy report

          How often Archivist puts a wrong value in the database. Measured once, on pages it had never seen, against targets written down before the test.

          Read once with no checks, about 45 in every 1,000 values went into the database wrong. Archivist let through about 4 in 1,000, twelve times fewer, by sending about 1 in 5 values to a person.

          Five targets, set before the test

          Written down and published before Archivist saw a single test page. Three were met, and two were missed narrowly.

            Checking more by hand means fewer mistakes

            Measured at the two marked points. Between them this mockup draws a smooth guess; the real app draws the measured curve.

            Worn pages get checked more

            Share of values sent to a person, by the condition of the page. Most of the checking goes to the most worn pages.

            Results come after the hand check

            58 pages from the US Treasury Bulletin, 1940 to 1942 and 1985 to 1994, read under rules written down before the run. A person is checking 400 of the values by hand, and this tab fills in once they’re done.

            It will show the same things as the test pages: wrong values per 1,000, the share sent to a person, and each target met or missed. It also names two things real archives add: pages whose layout changed during the war, and tables that gained or lost rows over the years.
